# Related work

The model applies one identical phase encoding to each of $`N`$ probes. The receiver gets the erasure locations and loses access to the erased states. The main benchmark is local quantum Fisher information (QFI) at a prescribed loss count, optimized over every input state and any protected register. A second benchmark averages over a known count distribution. The contributions are a sharp bound and coherent readout that attains it, plus conditions that make protected memory necessary for exact attainment.

## Closest published ingredients and comparisons

| Source | Established result | Relation to the present result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E. L. Lehmann and G. Casella, *Theory of Point Estimation*, 2nd ed., §5.1 Example 1.17, Eqs. (1.43)–(1.45). [Text](https://www.dcpehvpm.org/E-Content/Stat/E%20L%20Lehaman.pdf) | The finite-population hypergeometric proportion problem has a constant-risk linear shrinkage estimator. Its least favorable distribution is beta-binomial. | Both the shrinkage rule and its attaining prior are inherited. The quantum converse identifies four times this squared-error risk for the total charge. The tagged Dicke probe and survivor-only syndrome readout realize it without physical access to the erased systems. |
| P. Amrhein, *Minimax Estimation of Proportions under Random Sample Size*, JASA **90** (1995). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1995.10476614) | Studies a classical multivariate hypergeometric experiment with ancillary sample size, and develops a minimax estimator within its considered class. Its abstract explicitly distinguishes the fixed-size estimator. | Random sample size and classical shrinkage have antecedents. The local quantum result optimizes over probes, uses one common coherent beta-binomial preparation across all counts and achieves the weighted QFI objective through a joint readout. |
| Y. Ouyang and N. Rengaswamy, *Describing quantum metrology with erasure errors using weight distributions of classical codes*, Phys. Rev. A **107**, 022620 (2023), Theorem 4 and Corollary 6. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02859) | Bounds QFI of probes derived from shortened classical codes using conditional weight variances. Concatenation achieves Heisenberg-order QFI at fixed $`t`$ and can tolerate long bursts under their specified conditions. | Passive robustness, erased-string branches and charge variances are established ideas. The present fixed-count result gives an optimal value over all probes, an attaining family for every $`N,t`$, and a register-dimension necessity in the few-survivor regime. Adversarial fixed-count erasure allows any subset of the specified cardinality. |
| Y. Ouyang, N. Shettell and D. Markham, *Robust quantum metrology with explicit symmetric states*, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory **68** (2022), Theorem 2. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02378) | Gives explicit gnu-code probes built from spaced Dicke weights and QFI lower bounds for fixed-count erasure. | The untagged one-erasure construction at even $`N`$ is a known three-weight gnu probe. The exact all-probe QFI benchmark, the dense $`N=3`$ overlapping-branch example, and the general fixed-$`t`$ sparse construction by exact moment matching address different properties. |
| R. Demkowicz-Dobrzański et al., *Quantum phase estimation with lossy interferometers*, Phys. Rev. A **80**, 013825 (2009), Sec. IV B. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.0456) | The optical two-arm-loss calculation upper-bounds QFI by distinguishing detailed arm-loss branches. Branches of equal total loss can overlap when both arms lose photons. | At equal arm transmissivities and conditioned on total loss, the optical symmetric sector has the same hypergeometric loss kernel as the symmetric spin model. The variance-refinement bound is inherited. Here a protected modular tag makes distinct erased charges coherently distinguishable and gives the finite closed form. |
| R. Demkowicz-Dobrzański and L. Maccone, *Using entanglement against noise in quantum metrology*, Phys. Rev. Lett. **113**, 250801 (2014), Eqs. (4), (5) and (15). [arXiv v3 full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.2934) | Uses independent, state-independent qubit erasure with an orthogonal flag. Its channel bound $`N\eta/(1-\eta)`$ is asymptotically attained without ancillas. | The independent-erasure case in this project uses the same parallel channel and protected-register comparison. At transmissivity $`1/2`$, one protected qubit gives a strict finite gap between their strategies (ii) and (iii) for three sensors. In arXiv v3, the text after Eq. (1) states finite equality for erasure. The finite example qualifies that statement while preserving the shared asymptotic limit proved using Eq. (15). |
| M. Arieli, A. Retzker and T. Gefen, *Optimal quantum metrology protocols with erasure qubits*, arXiv:2603.11807. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11807) | Analyzes detected erasure in a continuous-time sensing model across several control classes. It optimizes interrogation and detection schedules, gives asymptotically attaining squeezed states for longitudinal sensing and studies passive finite probes numerically. | Continuous monitoring gives access to jump timing and allows time-domain controls. The fixed-count benchmark here has a single parallel use per sensor, only the final location flags and a closed finite optimum. |
| J. Sheng, *Finite-Syndrome Compression of Quantum Fisher Information*, arXiv:2608.02333. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02333) | Formulates the QFI loss from compressing an existing fine-grained classical–quantum record in terms of its conditional SLD scores. | Common-SLD equality is related to this score condition and follows here by completing the square. The probe and coherent syndrome measurement create a readable erased-charge label out of surviving quantum systems. The memory lower bound uses the eigenvectors required by distinct conditional charge means. The comparison to Sheng was verified from the abstract and indexed text. |
